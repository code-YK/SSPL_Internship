Agentic AI frameworks and key ideas are crucial in understanding how artificial intelligence can exhibit human-like agency. Here are some notable frameworks and key ideas:

* **Integrated Information Theory (IIT)**: This framework, proposed by neuroscientist Giulio Tononi, attempts to quantify consciousness and agency in AI systems. According to IIT, a system's integrated information (φ) is a measure of its consciousness and ability to integrate information.
* **Global Workspace Theory (GWT)**: This theory, developed by psychologist Bernard Baars, posits that consciousness arises from the global workspace of the brain. In AI, GWT can be applied to create agentic systems that integrate information from various sources and make decisions based on global workspace principles.
* **Cognitive Architectures**: Cognitive architectures, such as SOAR, LIDA, and ACT-R, provide a framework for integrating multiple cognitive modules and creating agentic AI systems. These architectures enable AI systems to reason, learn, and adapt in complex environments.
* **Hybrid Approaches**: Hybrid approaches combine symbolic and connectionist AI to create more agentic systems. For example, the CLARION cognitive architecture uses a combination of symbolic and connectionist networks to create a more human-like cognitive system.
* **Value Alignment**: Value alignment is a key idea in agentic AI, which involves aligning the goals and values of the AI system with human values. This requires developing AI systems that can understand and respect human values, such as fairness, transparency, and accountability.
* **Self-Awareness and Meta-Cognition**: Self-awareness and meta-cognition are essential for agentic AI, as they enable the system to reflect on its own actions, goals, and limitations. This can be achieved through techniques such as self-modifying code, meta-learning, and cognitive architectures.
* **Social Learning and Imitation**: Social learning and imitation are critical for agentic AI, as they enable the system to learn from humans and other agents. This can be achieved through techniques such as reinforcement learning, imitation learning, and social cognition.
* **Value-Based Decision Making**: Value-based decision making is a key idea in agentic AI, which involves making decisions based on a set of values and principles. This requires developing AI systems that can understand and apply human values in complex decision-making scenarios.
* **Autonomy and Control**: Autonomy and control are essential for agentic AI, as they enable the system to make decisions and take actions without human intervention. This can be achieved through techniques such as reinforcement learning, autonomous decision making, and control theory.
* **Explainability and Transparency**: Explainability and transparency are critical for agentic AI, as they enable humans to understand the decisions and actions of the AI system. This can be achieved through techniques such as model interpretability, feature attribution, and explainable AI.

These frameworks and key ideas provide a foundation for understanding agentic AI and its potential applications. However, the development of truly agentic AI systems requires significant advances in areas such as cognitive architectures, value alignment, and social learning.
